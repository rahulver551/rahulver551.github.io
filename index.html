Research direction

Computer vision, multimodal, agentic, and distributed intelligence.

I am interested in visual models that learn from incomplete supervision, multimodal systems that connect images, audio, language, graphs, and sensor evidence, agents that reason and use tools without losing provenance, and distributed learning systems that operate under privacy, latency, memory, and communication constraints.

My IIT Madras research began with a practical constraint: network providers could not share private traffic data. I studied federated forecasting, in which each provider trains locally and shares model updates, and then used the forecasts to investigate when to scale services and where to place them. The published experiments used public traces and synthetic data to represent separate providers.

Experience & selected projects

Cisco Systems

Agentic systems, network intelligence, engineering AI, and edge platforms.

01

AgenticOps: Autonomous Network Reasoning

When a network fails, an engineer may need to piece together measurements, device logs, configuration changes, and past incidents. I designed agents that gather this evidence, test possible causes, verify a diagnosis, and prepare a response. A graph of devices and their connections helps the agents follow real dependencies. Human approval, rollback, and post-action checks guard any proposed change. For targeted incidents, diagnosis fell from about 60 minutes to 5 minutes while active token use fell by more than 40%.

02

Long-Audio QA for Data-Center Equipment

An alarm tone or change in fan noise may last only seconds within hours of data-center recordings, yet help an engineer investigate a fault. I adapted and deployed a long-audio question-answering pipeline for this use case: detected sounds are indexed as timestamped events, an engineer's question retrieves relevant records and recording intervals, and the answer is grounded in that audio evidence instead of the entire recording.

03

Graph RAG Agent for PHY/Port Debugging

Built a graph-guided assistant for physical network faults. It connects a port to the hardware components behind it, combines that map with operational data, and helps engineers trace causes of unstable links or corrupted traffic. This is a separate, hardware-focused investigation tool from AgenticOps.

04

AI-Powered Code Review Platform

Built specialized LLM agents for bugs, security, performance, and maintainability, grounded by code knowledge graphs, semantic embeddings, and static-analysis signals. Used confidence-weighted aggregation and developer feedback to improve cross-file reasoning and finding quality.

05

Edge AI Hardware Power Fingerprinting

Learned device-specific signatures from PoE power, current, and voltage telemetry through Cisco IOx and Isolation Forest to detect malicious hardware swaps and automate closed-loop port isolation.

06

Hot-Pluggable Edge Intelligence Platform

Helped develop a hot-pluggable compute module that adds capacity to an industrial network switch for LLM and vision applications. I worked on shared-storage access, container support, data paths, telemetry, and workload benchmarking so AI services could run close to the network.

07

Frame Preemption for Camera Traffic

Implemented IEEE 802.1Qbu frame preemption for high-priority camera traffic. If a lower-priority frame is already crossing the link, the switch pauses it, sends the camera frame, then resumes the interrupted transmission. I worked on firmware, express and preempted traffic queues, per-port configuration, and link-partner negotiation. This reduces the wait for visual data used by time-sensitive applications.

08

Object Selection for On-Device Image Editing

At OnePlus, I worked on the visual-selection stage of a mobile editing feature: identifying the object a user wanted removed and segmenting the pixels that belonged to it. An inaccurate boundary would remain visible in the finished image, even if the missing background was synthesized well. The work required attention to visual quality within a phone's memory and response-time limits.

10

FathomNet Positive-Unlabeled Marine Object Detection

In the FathomNet-CLEF 2026 Kaggle challenge, many visible marine animals were not annotated; treating them as background could teach a detector to miss real objects. I implemented a positive-unlabeled pipeline that expanded training supervision, filtered candidate pseudo-labels, combined complementary detectors, and merged predictions across image resolutions. I also proposed a controlled implicit-neural-representation experiment to test whether it helps detect small organisms across scales; that experiment is a proposal, not a reported result.

11

Extractive Question Answering with DistilBERT

Fine-tuned DistilBERT on SpokenSQuAD using linear learning-rate decay and automatic mixed precision, improving test F1 from 52.25 to 54.05.

12

GAN Comparison: DCGAN vs. WGAN vs. ACGAN

Implemented and compared three GAN architectures on CIFAR-10 across 40 epochs. DCGAN achieved the best FID of 206.19 versus 259.59 for WGAN and 281.99 for ACGAN.
